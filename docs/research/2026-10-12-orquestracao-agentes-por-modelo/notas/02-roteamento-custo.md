# LLM model routing, cascading and tiering for cost/token optimization (focus: code generation and coding agents)

Research date: 2026-10-12. Prices quoted are from Anthropic's pricing page fetched on that date. Items marked (vendor-internal) are the vendor's own evals; items marked (opinion/inference) are mine, not measured.

## 1. Key papers and systems: what savings and quality deltas were measured?

### Takeaway
General-purpose routers and cascades (FrugalGPT, RouteLLM, cascade routing) report large savings (45-98%) at near-parity quality, but those figures are best-case, benchmark-specific, and depend on a good quality estimator. Tested on repository-level coding, prompt-only routers do poorly. Routers that look at the partial trajectory or a verified handoff do better. Multi-model gains in coding are real but much smaller than chat-benchmark headlines.

### Cited Findings
- FrugalGPT (Chen, Zaharia, Zou, 2023): LLM API fees differ by about two orders of magnitude. The learned cascade can match the best single LLM (e.g. GPT-4) with "up to 98%" cost reduction, or improve accuracy by 4% at equal cost. The abstract gives no per-dataset detail. — [arXiv 2305.05176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176)
- FrugalGPT caveat: needs labeled data and compute to train the response scorer, and benchmark results may not transfer to real workloads. — [TechTalks summary via search](https://bdtechtalks.com/2023/06/12/reduce-costs-of-chatgpt-gpt-4/)
- RouteLLM (Ong et al., 2024): routers trained on human-preference data choose between a strong and a weak model. Headline: over 85% cost reduction on MT-Bench, 45% on MMLU and 35% on GSM8K versus GPT-4-only, while keeping 95% of GPT-4 performance. Model pair: GPT-4 Turbo and Mixtral 8x7B. — [LMSYS blog](https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-07-01-routellm/); abstract: [arXiv 2406.18665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18665)
- RouteLLM caveats: the detailed numbers are measured against a random-routing baseline. The MT-Bench 85% corresponds to about 14% GPT-4 calls (with LLM-judge data augmentation) and the cost model treats the weak model as about free. Routers trained on Arena data alone were near random on MMLU. MMLU gains needed about 1,500 in-domain golden-label samples (45% savings there, and only about 14% cheaper than random in the detailed table). Transfer to Claude 3 Opus / Llama 3 8B was tested only on MT-Bench. — [LMSYS blog](https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-07-01-routellm/)
- Cascade routing (Dekoninck, Baader, Vechev, ICML 2025): derives an optimal cascading strategy, proves an existing routing strategy optimal, and unifies the two ("cascade routing"). It "consistently outperforms" routing or cascading alone, and good quality estimators are "the critical factor" for success. No numbers on the abstract page. — [PMLR v267](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/dekoninck25a.html)
- Mixture-of-Agents (Wang et al., 2024): layered ensembles of LLMs, each layer seeing the previous layer's outputs. A secondary search summary reports a 65.8% AlpacaEval 2.0 win rate versus 57.5% for GPT-4 Omni. A different third-party summary says 65.1%, so the exact figure is unverified. MoA multiplies calls per query, so it is a quality technique, not a cost-saving one. — [arXiv 2406.04692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04692)
- "AI Agents That Matter" (Kapoor, Stroebl et al., Princeton, TMLR 2025): accuracy-only agent leaderboards are misleading. Simple baselines matched complex agents at much lower cost (a summary cites up to 50x cost gaps). It argues for plotting accuracy against cost (Pareto) and optimizing both. — [project page](https://agents.cs.princeton.edu/); [summary](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/new-paper-ai-agents-that-matter)
- "Small Language Models are the Future of Agentic AI" (NVIDIA, Belcak et al., 2025): a position paper (explicitly a "value statement", not an experiment). It argues agent systems mostly use LMs for narrow, repetitive subtasks, so SLMs suffice for many calls. It recommends heterogeneous systems with occasional LLM calls and outlines an LLM-to-SLM conversion algorithm. — [arXiv 2506.02153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02153)
- Agentless (Xia et al., 2024): a fixed pipeline (hierarchical localization, then repair with 40 candidate patches, then test-based filtering) with no autonomous planning. It resolved 96/300 (32.00%) of SWE-bench Lite at $0.70 average (about 78k tokens) with GPT-4o. In the same table: OpenHands CodeAct 26.67% ($1.14), Aider 26.33%, SWE-agent with Claude 3.5 Sonnet 23.00% ($1.62). The v1 paper said 27.33% at $0.34, so quote the version. Authors' critique of agents: imprecise tool use wastes calls, self-directed planning is hard to control (30-40 turn trajectories), and agents struggle to self-reflect so early errors compound. — [arXiv 2407.01489v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01489v2); version history via [search summary](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01489v1)
- SWE-Router (Son et al., ICML 2026 DL4Code workshop, arXiv Jun 30 2026): a cheap model runs K exploratory turns, then a value head (Qwen2.5-Coder-7B + LoRA) reads the partial trajectory and decides whether to continue or restart on the strong model. Route-AUC on SWE-bench Verified (0 = cost-proportional, 1 = strong-model resolve rate at weak-model cost): deepseek-v3.2 to gemini-3-pro 0.780 (K=2) versus 0.627 for a non-temporal (prompt-only) router. gpt-5-mini to gemini-3-pro 0.709 (K=4) versus 0.565 for the best prompt-only baseline. The paper does not give a headline percentage saving. It argues prompt-only routing hits a Bayes-error floor because a typo fix and a multi-module refactor can look alike in the issue text. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00053); details via [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.00053.md)
- Scrouting / SuperScout (SuperAGI, arXiv Aug 5 2026; vendor research, single benchmark): replaying learned routers over public per-task results, solve sets are strongly nested. The tasks a weaker model solves are largely a subset of the strongest model's (containment 0.941 on SWE-bench Verified, 0.912 on Multilingual, 0.773 on Pro). No learned router beat "always pick the strongest" on solve rate. So routing pays on cost, not accuracy. On SWE-bench Pro Python (266 tasks), a 7B scout writes a sandbox-verified handoff (about 4 KB: implicated files, repro attempt, dead ends), then a router picks one of four frontier fixers. Result: 159/266 solved versus 158 for the best single model, at $0.230 versus $1.274 total cost per solve (about one fifth). Important ablation: "always the cheapest fixer with the handoff" tied the routed system, so the verified handoff, not the routing decision, carried the result. Per-fixer effects were directional only (N=99). — [arXiv 2608.04804](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04804) (PDF text extracted locally)
- Token-use study (Bai et al., 2026, 8 frontier LLMs, SWE-bench Verified, OpenHands): agentic coding uses about 1,000x more tokens than code chat or reasoning. Input tokens (not output) drive most cost. The same task can vary up to 30x in tokens across runs. Accuracy often peaks at intermediate cost then plateaus. Models predict their own token use poorly (correlation up to 0.39) and underestimate. — [arXiv 2604.22750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22750)

### Inferences
- (inference) For coding, the "nested solve set" result suggests the cost lever is correctly identifying the easy subset, not combining model strengths. That makes cheap, trustworthy verification (tests, types) the key enabling asset.
- (inference) Chat-benchmark savings (RouteLLM 85%) should not be transferred to coding agents. The coding-agent evidence shows prompt-only routers underperform trajectory- or handoff-aware ones.

### Gaps
- I could not read SWE-Router's absolute cost-saving percentages (they are only in a figure). Only Route-AUC is confirmed.
- No peer-reviewed independent replication of Scrouting. It is vendor research.
- I did not retrieve the full FrugalGPT or RouteLLM per-dataset tables.

## 2. Evidence specific to coding agents (Aider architect/editor, mixed-model SWE results, vendor guidance)

### Takeaway
Aider's architect/editor split showed measurable gains, but mostly when the architect is a reasoning model that formats edits badly. Pairing a model with itself also helped, and later results show the split does not always help. Anthropic's own current guidance is more cautious than the "use Haiku everywhere" folklore: multi-model setups must beat the same model at lower effort, and most measured configurations did not save money.

### Cited Findings
- Aider architect/editor (Sept 2024, aider code-editing benchmark): o1-preview as architect with o1-mini or DeepSeek as editor (whole format) reached 85.0% (SOTA then). o1-preview with Claude 3.5 Sonnet as editor (diff) reached 82.7%, against 79.7% for o1-preview alone. Claude 3.5 Sonnet as architect with DeepSeek editor: 78.9%, against 77.4% alone. Sonnet with itself as editor: 80.5%. GPT-4o with itself: 75.2% versus 71.4%. GPT-4o-mini with itself: 60.2% versus 55.6%. o1-mini with DeepSeek editor (whole): 71.4% versus 61.1% alone. Stated motivation: o1-class models reason well but fail to produce correctly formatted edit instructions, so a second model does the edit formatting. DeepSeek was "effective" as a cheap editor with the diff format, improving every architect's baseline except Sonnet's. — [aider.chat architect post](https://aider.chat/2024/09/26/architect.html)
- Aider R1 + Sonnet (Jan 2025, polyglot benchmark): R1 architect with Sonnet editor 64.0% correct, 100% correct edit format, total $13.29. Solo: o1 61.7% ($186.5), R1 56.9% ($5.42), Sonnet 51.6% ($14.41), DeepSeek V3 48.4% ($0.34). The post says o1 + Sonnet did not beat o1 alone, and other editors did not appear to improve o1 or R1 over their solo scores, unlike the earlier o1-preview/o1-mini result. — [aider.chat R1+Sonnet](https://aider.chat/2025/01/24/r1-sonnet.html)
- Aider leaderboard (last updated 2025-11-20): the architect entries are o3 (high) + gpt-4.1 at 78.2% ($17.55) and R1 + claude-3-5-sonnet at 64.0% ($13.29). Solo entries for comparison: gpt-5 (high) 88.0% ($29.08), gpt-5 (low) 81.3% ($10.37), o3 (high) 81.3% ($21.23). No Claude Haiku/Sonnet-editor architect pairing from the current Claude generation was found. — [aider leaderboards](https://aider.chat/docs/leaderboards/)
- Anthropic multi-agent research system (Opus 4 lead, Sonnet 4 subagents): the multi-agent setup beat single-agent Opus 4 by 90.2% on an internal research eval. Agents use about 4x chat tokens, multi-agent about 15x. Token usage explained 80% of BrowseComp performance variance. Anthropic says multi-agent is a poor fit for work where agents share the same context, with many inter-agent dependencies, "most coding tasks" (fewer parallelizable subtasks), and low-value tasks. — [Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- Anthropic advisor strategy (Apr 9 2026, vendor-internal): a cheap executor (Sonnet or Haiku) runs the loop and consults Opus on hard decisions, with the advisor returning only about 400-700 tokens per call. Sonnet 4.6 + Opus advisor on SWE-bench Multilingual: +2.7 points (74.8% vs 72.1%) with cost per task down 11.9%. Caveat from Anthropic's footnotes: not like-for-like (solo run used adaptive thinking, advisor run had thinking off). Haiku 4.5 + Opus advisor on BrowseComp: 41.2% vs 19.7% solo, "85% cheaper per task" than Sonnet solo while trailing it. A customer (Eve Legal) reported Haiku 4.5 + Opus 4.6 advisor matched frontier quality on structured extraction at 5x lower cost. — [Anthropic blog](https://claude.com/blog/the-advisor-strategy); figures also in [search summary](https://claude.com/resources/articles/the-advisor-strategy)
- Anthropic "Optimizing for cost and intelligence" (current docs, vendor-internal, "directional"): the advisor pays only if the executor actually consults. With no prompt asking for it, Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 called an Opus 5.5 advisor on 0 of 198 questions, so the tool definition (about 1,000 prompt tokens) only added 12% (Haiku) to 25% (Sonnet) cost. On an internal agentic-coding benchmark, an Opus 5.5 executor + Fable 5.1 advisor scored 90.1% at $2.92 per attempt, 1.7 points above Opus 5.5 alone at about 2.1x the cost. Opus 5.5 alone at `xhigh` effort scored 91.1% for $4.11, "so the advisor buys roughly what more effort buys". In the orchestrator pattern, "money was saved in only two measured situations": (1) insurance against the cost tail on routine work (Fable 5 + one Sonnet 5 worker cost about half of Fable 5 alone on an easy BrowseComp slice, about a third at p90, but on the full set Fable 5 alone matched at 22-30% lower cost), and (2) work larger than one context window (21.6M-token corpus: Fable 5.1 lead + 25 Sonnet 5 workers cost 47-55% less but scored 10-12 points lower). In all other cases the orchestrator's own model at lower effort was cheaper. "Sweep effort on your current model first." — [Anthropic docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)
- Anthropic docs positioning: Haiku 5.5 is "the lowest latency and price", with "sub-agent tasks" listed as a use case. Sonnet 5.5 is for "everyday coding" and Opus 5.5 for "complex agentic coding... large-scale refactoring". The docs offer two starting paths (efficiency-first from Haiku, capability-first from Opus) and say effort tuning "is often a better lever than switching models". — [Choosing a model](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/choosing-a-model)
- Claude Code subagent guidance: set `model: haiku` (or sonnet/opus/inherit) per subagent. "Route read-heavy or exploratory work to a lower-cost model such as Haiku." The built-in Explore subagent can be overridden by a user-defined `Explore` with `model: haiku`. Subagents count toward the same usage limits. — [Claude Code subagents docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents)
- Haiku 5.5 coding numbers (secondary source, unverified against Anthropic's page): SWE-bench Pro 64.8% versus 81.3% for Sonnet 5.5, and 83.7% on SWE-bench Multilingual (max effort). Anthropic reportedly did not publish SWE-bench Verified for Haiku 5.5. As of Oct 8 2026 one source says no independent lab results yet. — [ComputingForgeeks via search](https://computingforgeeks.com/claude-haiku-5-5-released-features-benchmarks/)

### Inferences
- (inference) Aider's results support "splitting reasoning from edit-formatting", not "cheap editor for everything". The cheap editor (DeepSeek) helped with weaker architects and as a drop-in, but results with newer reasoning models were mixed.
- (inference) For the repo's manager/executor setup, Anthropic's own data says the split pays mainly when there is bulk, parallel, independent work or a cost-tail to cap. A single dependent chain is better served by one model at tuned effort. Per-item work packets in a wave are closer to the "independent pieces" case.

### Gaps
- No Aider-style architect/editor benchmark with the current Haiku/Sonnet/Opus generations. The Aider leaderboard data is dated Nov 2025 and predates them.
- No independent (non-vendor) SWE-bench results mixing Haiku-class workers with Opus-class planners.
- I did not find a published cost-per-resolved-issue comparison across Claude tiers from a neutral party.

## 3. Task properties that predict small-model sufficiency, and published routing criteria

### Takeaway
No published, validated checklist exists. The evidence points to: a small model suffices when the task is localized, fully specified and cheaply verifiable. A strong model is needed when difficulty is hidden until exploration, when the task spans modules, or when the check is itself a judgment. Verified handoffs (localization plus a reproduction test) appear to be what lets cheap models succeed.

### Cited Findings
- Prompt text alone is a weak predictor of difficulty in agentic coding: similar issue descriptions can imply a typo fix or a multi-module refactor. A few exploratory turns improve routing (Route-AUC 0.627 to 0.780). — [SWE-Router](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00053); [details](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.00053.md)
- Human-rated difficulty correlates only weakly with actual token cost, and models poorly predict their own usage. So LLM self-routing or human labels on difficulty are unreliable inputs. — [Bai et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22750)
- Cheaper models' solved tasks are largely nested within the strongest model's (0.94, 0.91, 0.77 containment). The tasks where cheap models suffice are a subset of the easy ones, and benefit is in cost not accuracy. — [Scrouting](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04804)
- A short (about 4 KB) verified handoff (files and line regions, repro command, dead ends) raised the cheaper fixers' solve rates, and the no-router ablation using the cheapest fixer with that handoff tied the routed system (N=99 calibration, directional). — [Scrouting](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04804)
- Agentless's fixed-pipeline design performed competitively with agents because localization and test-based filtering are explicit steps. It did worse than closed-source agents when issues had no location clues. — [Agentless v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01489v2)
- Quality estimator is the "critical factor" for routing/cascade success. — [Dekoninck et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/dekoninck25a.html)
- Verifier reliability limits cascades: LLM-judge code verification struggles on harder problems, which are exactly the ones that need escalation (ICLR 2026 submission, per search summary). Structured unit-test suites gave the strongest filtering gains in a code-training study. — [ICLR 2026 listing](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10012641); [arXiv 2509.20837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.20837)
- Anthropic: advisor pattern is a poor fit when "every turn needs frontier capability", when there is nothing to plan, or when the executor is already close to the advisor. Delegation is a poor fit for "one dependent chain, or work that fits in a single context". The capability gap must exist for an advisor to help. — [Anthropic docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)
- Anthropic positions Haiku 5.5 for narrow tasks (compaction, summarization, classification, subagents) per a secondary analysis, and recommends Sonnet/Opus for complex agentic coding. — [ComputingForgeeks via search](https://computingforgeeks.com/claude-haiku-5-5-released-features-benchmarks/); [Anthropic choosing-a-model](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/choosing-a-model)

### Inferences
- (opinion/inference, no published rubric found) A routing rubric consistent with the evidence:
  - Cheap-eligible: signature/types and acceptance tests already exist (or are in the packet); change touches one file or one module boundary; no new design decision; failure is detectable by a deterministic check (test/typecheck/lint); the packet fits in a small context.
  - Strong-required: ambiguous or conflicting spec; cross-module or cross-aggregate invariants; deciding where code goes; writing the tests that define "done"; review and judging of equivalent-looking implementations (LLM-judge verification is weak on hard problems); integration and merge.
- (inference) The framework's conventions (stable invariant IDs, `covers([...])` tests, `notImplemented()` skeletons, `agentic packet`/`verify`) are exactly the "fully specified + deterministically verifiable" properties the literature says make cheap workers viable. The handoff-quality finding (Scrouting) suggests packet quality is the main lever.

### Gaps
- No published, validated rubric or classifier for coding-subtask routing beyond the learned routers above. I could not confirm per-task-type success rates (e.g. "unit-test-guided function implementation: Haiku passes X%").
- I did not find an ablation on how much spec detail a cheap model needs.

## 4. Cost model: estimating a multi-agent pipeline versus a single agent (with current pricing)

### Takeaway
Cost is dominated by input tokens (re-reading context each turn), so what matters is tokens-per-tier times cache behavior. As of 2026-10-12, Anthropic's list prices put Haiku 5.5 at 1/20 of Sonnet 5.5 and 1/40 of Opus 5.5, but Sonnet-to-Opus is only 2x. So the cheap-worker gain is large only when the worker really is Haiku-class and the job does not need retries on the stronger tier.

### Cited Findings
- Anthropic pricing page (fetched 2026-10-12; USD per MTok; input / cache-write 5m / cache-write 1h / cache-read / output):
  - Claude Haiku 5.5, prompts up to 100k tokens: 0.10 / 0.125 / 0.20 / 0.01 / 0.50. Over 100k tokens: 0.50 / 0.625 / 1 / 0.05 / 2.50 (priced per request by prompt length, counting cache reads and writes).
  - Claude Haiku 4.5: 1 / 1.25 / 2 / 0.10 / 5.
  - Claude Sonnet 5.5: 2 / 2.50 / 4 / 0.10 (0.05x) / 10. Sonnet 5: 2 / 2.50 / 4 / 0.20 / 10 (the $2/$10 introductory price became permanent). Sonnet 4.6 and 4.5: 3 / 3.75 / 6 / 0.30 / 15.
  - Claude Opus 5.5: 4 / 5 / 8 / 0.20 (0.05x) / 20. Opus 5, 4.8, 4.7, 4.6, 4.5: 5 / 6.25 / 10 / 0.50 / 25. Opus 4.1 and 4 (retired except on some clouds): 15 / 18.75 / 30 / 1.50 / 75.
  - Claude Fable 5.1 (top tier open to all): 10 / 12.50 / 20 / 0.25 / 50.
  - Batch API: 50% discount on input and output; it does not apply to fast mode or Managed Agents sessions. Fast mode on Opus is premium-priced. Claude 4.7 and later models use a tokenizer that yields about 30% more tokens for the same text, so per-token price comparisons across generations understate real cost differences. — [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)
- Prompt caching: 5-minute write 1.25x, 1-hour write 2x, read 0.1x (0.05x for Opus 5.5/Sonnet 5.5, 0.025x for Fable/Mythos 5.1). Default TTL 5 minutes, refreshed free on each hit. Hits require an exact prefix match, and tools, then system, then messages invalidate in order. The docs page does not state whether caches are shared across models, but a prefix cache is tied to the prompt for a specific model, so a different-model worker should be assumed to start with a cold cache (inference). Minimum cacheable prompt: 512 tokens for Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5, 4,096 for Haiku 4.5. — [Prompt caching docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)
- Fresh-context overhead: non-fork Claude Code subagents start with an isolated context. They get their system prompt, the task message, CLAUDE.md files and a git status snapshot, but not the conversation history, skills already invoked, or files already read. Forks inherit the full conversation and share the parent's prompt cache. Each subagent spends its own tokens and its returned results add to the main context. — [Claude Code subagents docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents)
- Token multipliers from Anthropic: agents about 4x chat, multi-agent about 15x chat. — [Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- Agentic coding is input-dominated (up to 1,000x chat tokens; one secondary source cites an input:output ratio above 150:1; same-task token variance up to 30x). One vendor example reports cache reads were about 94.5% of one Claude Code session's tokens (a single session, not a study). — [Bai et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22750); [token-economics survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09104); [Augment guide](https://www.augmentcode.com/guides/ai-coding-cost-analysis-agent-token-spend)
- Orchestrator accounting from Anthropic's corpus experiment: the Fable 5.1 + 25 Sonnet 5 workers system used about 560M cached tokens per episode versus about 365M for the solo model, yet cost 47-55% less because nearly all tokens were at the worker's cache-read rate. The advisor tool definition alone added about 1,000 prompt tokens per request. Advisor calls cost output at advisor rates (400-700 tokens typical). — [Anthropic docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence); [advisor blog](https://claude.com/blog/the-advisor-strategy)
- Yardstick used by Scrouting: total cost per solve at matched solve rate, counting every component (searcher GPU, sandboxes, API), compared against the blind-mixing line (random split between cheap and strong). — [Scrouting](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04804)

### Inferences
- (inference, my arithmetic from the list prices above) Tier ratios as of 2026-10-12: Haiku 5.5 : Sonnet 5.5 : Opus 5.5 = 1 : 20 : 40 on both input and output (for prompts up to 100k; the Haiku 5.5 over-100k price is 0.50/2.50, so Haiku is then only 1:4 to Sonnet 5.5). Previous generation: Haiku 4.5 : Sonnet 4.6 : Opus 4.6 = 1 : 3 : 5. The tier gap changed sharply with generation, so any routing break-even must be recomputed per model release.
- (inference) Pipeline cost sketch: C_pipeline = C_plan(strong) + sum over packets of [C_pack_tokens(cold-cache input) + C_worker(turns x avg context x cache-read price + new input + output)] x (1 + retry rate) + P(fail after N retries) x C_escalate(strong, from fresh or with failure log) + C_review(strong). C_single = turns x growing context at one tier. Items that raise handoff cost: instruction and tool prefix re-sent per subagent (uncached on first call at 1.25x write), packet text, returned summaries re-read by the manager on every later turn.
- (inference) Break-even for try-cheap-then-escalate, assuming a cheap deterministic verifier and the strong model redoing the task in full after failure: cheap-first is cheaper in expectation when p_pass x C_strong > C_cheap_attempt (+ verification), i.e. roughly p_pass > C_cheap/C_strong. At a 1:20 price ratio that is a pass rate of only about 5% before accounting for token-count differences. At 1:3 (previous generation) about 33%. Real thresholds are higher because weaker models often use more tokens per task, retries multiply cost, and false passes (tests green, wrong behavior) shift cost to review. Bai et al. show token use varies up to 30x per task, so use measured medians and tails, not a single estimate.
- (inference) Cache effects: a single long-lived strong agent benefits from cache reads at 0.05-0.1x on its growing context. Spawning workers on a different model forfeits that cache for each fresh context, so workers with large preloaded context erode savings. Keep packets small and self-contained. Haiku 5.5's per-request price jump above 100k prompt tokens makes it a poor choice for large-context workers.
- (inference) Because "Sonnet-to-Opus" is only 2x at current list prices while effort tuning moves cost more than model choice, Anthropic's advice to price the stronger model at low effort first is a real alternative to a 3-tier pipeline.

### Gaps
- No published end-to-end token accounting of a manager/worker coding pipeline with handoff overhead measured (the closest is Anthropic's corpus experiment, which is read-heavy, not code generation).
- The prompt-caching page I read does not explicitly state cross-model cache sharing. I inferred cold caches across models.
- I did not verify per-turn overhead numbers (e.g. tokens in a Claude Code subagent system prompt).

## 5. Escalation policies when a cheap worker fails verification

### Takeaway
Published escalation designs fall into four types: restart on the strong model, strong model as on-demand advisor, cascade by confidence, and trajectory-conditioned escalation after a few cheap turns. Evidence is thin for coding specifically, and restart-from-scratch wastes the cheap model's work.

### Cited Findings
- Cascade (FrugalGPT): query models in cost order, stop when a learned scorer deems the answer reliable. Needs labeled data to train the scorer. — [FrugalGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176); [summary](https://bdtechtalks.com/2023/06/12/reduce-costs-of-chatgpt-gpt-4/)
- Optimal cascade vs routing, and combined cascade routing; quality estimator quality is decisive. — [Dekoninck et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/dekoninck25a.html)
- SWE-Router: weak model runs K=1-4 turns, a value head predicts success, and if below threshold the task restarts from scratch on the strong model. Scrouting's authors note that this means the probe's work is not consumed on escalation. — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.00053.md); [Scrouting](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04804)
- Scrouting avoids mid-task escalation: a single pass with a verified handoff from a small searcher to the chosen fixer. It filters false claims in the handoff in a sandbox before the fixer sees them (small models overclaimed reproductions; the gate strips them). — [Scrouting](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04804)
- Advisor tool: the cheap executor keeps the loop and calls the stronger model for plans, corrections or a stop signal, with `max_uses` caps. Anthropic suggests prompting for about two to three calls per task (before substantive work, before finishing) and says the consult rate is "the fragile part": in some configs it was 0 consults, and a low-effort executor can stop noticing it is stuck. If the executor asks on most tasks, "running the advisor's model directly is likely cheaper". — [Anthropic docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence); [advisor blog](https://claude.com/blog/the-advisor-strategy)
- CodeRescue (cited by Scrouting) routes among recovery actions (reflect, replan, escalate) inside one agent's trajectory. I only have this via Scrouting's related-work table. — [Scrouting](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04804)
- Anthropic recommends recheck at each model release, since releases change the capability gap and price ratio, and shadow-running the winning configuration on a traffic slice before cutover. — [Anthropic docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)

### Inferences
- (opinion/inference) A defensible policy for a manager/worker coding setup: (1) cheap worker attempts with a self-contained packet and a deterministic gate (tests, typecheck, lint); (2) on failure, one bounded retry on the same tier with the failure output appended; (3) then escalate to the next tier up, passing the packet, the failing output and a short summary of dead ends (do not discard cheap-tier evidence); (4) strong-tier review remains a separate cost line, since LLM-judging of hard code is unreliable. Cap total retries and track escalation rate per task class, which doubles as the routing signal. This is my synthesis, not a published, benchmarked policy.
- (inference) An escalation rate that climbs for a task class means the class should be routed to the stronger tier up front, per Anthropic's "measure the consult rate" advice and the break-even above.

### Gaps
- No published measurement comparing "restart on strong" versus "continue with strong plus failure log" in coding agents (Scrouting argues the latter conceptually, but tests no direct comparison).
- No data on optimal retry counts for cheap workers before escalation.
- I did not find Cursor/Devin/Copilot engineering write-ups with measured escalation thresholds.
